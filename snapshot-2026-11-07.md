## Notes

- Backend ids are not contiguous (2 and 3 are missing) — retired backends leave gaps; do not assume an id exists because the numbers run that far.
- The Ry emulator reports `is_hardware: true` in the API even though it is an emulator. The flag is the platform's, not mine — read the name, not just the field.
- Tuna-17 is the agreed first hardware run (3-qubit GHZ). On this probe it was benchmarking, not idle: a hardware run should re-check status first, not assume the slot is open.
- Offline does not mean empty: Tuna-5 and Tuna-9 each still hold queued jobs, with average waits measured in weeks. Jobs submitted to a backend that goes offline sit there; check the queue, not just the status, before submitting.

- **QX emulator** (id 1): Quantum Inspire offers a powerful quantum computer simulator backend. The QX Simulator can simulate up to 34 qubits (on the Lisa supercomputer) on a classical computer setup. It can execute quantum circuits and allows inspection of the simulated quantum state.
- **Tuna-5** (id 4): The Tuna-5 backend has 5 transmon qubits in a star-like configuration, with 4 flux-tunable couplers connecting nearest neighbors.
